## Overview

The skvader is one of the best-known Swedish 'tall-tale' or composite taxidermy creatures. Its origin lies in an early-20th-century dinner story by hunter Håkan Dahlmark of Sundsvall, who claimed to have shot the animal in 1874. In 1907 his housekeeper presented him with a painting of the creature, which he later donated to a local museum. In 1918 the museum asked taxidermist Rudolf Granberg to mount an actual specimen, which combined the head, forequarters, and hindlegs of a European hare (Lepus europaeus) with the back, wings, and tail of a female western capercaillie (Tetrao urogallus). The completed mount was given the joke Latin name Tetrao lepus pseudo-hybridus rarissimus and is permanently displayed at the museum at Norra Berget in Sundsvall, Sweden. The skvader is now an unofficial symbol of Sundsvall and of the Medelpad province, and the noun 'skvader' has entered Swedish as a colloquialism for an awkward combination of contradictory things.

## Etymology

- **Origin**: Swedish — a portmanteau coined for Granberg's taxidermy mount.
- **Meaning**: Combines 'skva-' from skvattra ('to chatter' / 'to chirp') and '-der' from tjäder ('western capercaillie / wood grouse').
- **First use**: 1918, when Granberg constructed the original specimen.

## Theories & Explanations

### Scientific

**Composite taxidermy artefact** _(Mainstream zoological consensus)_

The skvader is a deliberately constructed composite taxidermy specimen, not a biological hybrid. European hare and western capercaillie are not genetically compatible.

### Skeptical

**Tall-tale origin** _(Wikipedia / standard reference treatment)_

The skvader originates in an explicit tall tale by Håkan Dahlmark and was never claimed in serious zoological literature.
